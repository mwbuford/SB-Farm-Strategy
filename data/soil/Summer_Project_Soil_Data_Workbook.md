## 7) Citation-Ready Quotes — L2 Whole Site Design

Use these when citing the L2 design report directly. Page numbers refer to the PDF page labels in the report.

### L2-C1 — Whole-site design scope
**Source:** L2 Whole Site Design, Executive Summary, p. 8

> "7th Generation Design was invited by Sarah and Jonathan Graham to develop a whole-site design for the 84 acre property located at 370 Ortega Ridge Road in Montecito, CA."

> "The Graham family has communicated the following major goals: rehabilitate a degraded plot of land, develop regenerative land-based systems that are supported by the climate, a beautiful setting to relocate to in 5-10 years, and development of land-based income."

### L2-C2 — Report organization (Keyline Scale of Permanence)
**Source:** L2 Whole Site Design, Report Organization, p. 10

> "This report is largely organized according to the Keyline Scale of Permanence, presented in order from things human beings are least able to change down to those things that we have greater agency with."

> "Land Shape & Climate > Water > Access > Shelter > Fertility, Food, Forage, Fiber, and Fuel > Energy > Economy"

### L2-C3 — Proposed passive water-harvesting system (executive summary)
**Source:** L2 Whole Site Design, Executive Summary, p. 9–10

> "A site-wide passive water harvesting and drainage system that will intercept an average of 5.1 - 10.3 million gallons of water every year that is eroding the valleys and being lost from the property as runoff, spread it out to the drier ridges, and infiltrate it into the soil for the benefit of downslope plantings and groundwater levels."

> "36 passive water-harvesting swales with a combined length of 1.75 miles and a combined bank-full volume of 159,000 gallons (Section 2.1)."

> "7 passive water-harvesting infiltration basins with a combined surface area of 3,174 square feet and a combined bank-full volume of 36,000 gallons (Section 2.1)."

> "24 road-draining 'rolling dips', 29 geologically-armored spillways and drains, 17 biologically-armored spillways and drains, 13 geologically-armored energy dissipating pools, and 8 geologically-armored flow-spreading structures for non-erosively draining overflow water into, between, and from water harvesting features (Section 2.2)."

### L2-C4 — Swale system infiltration capacity (detailed design section)
**Source:** L2 Whole Site Design, Section 2.1 Swales, p. 54

> "The entire system as illustrated in the design map below consists of 32 swales with a combined length of 9,280 linear feet and, with the recommended infiltration basins (described in the next section), is capable of infiltrating an estimated 3.6 - 9 million gallons of water that is currently running off of the property during each average rain year, and will infiltrate all of that water into the soil in all but the most intense precipitation events."

> "At the Montecito Water District's current agricultural water rate of $.00760/gallon, this equates to $27,360 - $68,400 of water every year that would be retained in the soils, helping to flush accumulated salts and provide the base input required for a healthy, thriving soil ecology."

> "This system will completely starve the existing headcuts and gullies of additional inflow, allowing for the initiation of healing processes."

> **Note:** Executive Summary (p. 10) lists 36 swales; Section 2.1 (p. 54) lists 32 swales. Use the section-level figure for detailed design work; flag the discrepancy if citing totals.

### L2-C5 — Existing runoff and water-loss conditions
**Source:** L2 Whole Site Design, Section 2.1 Existing Conditions, p. 50–51

> "During a typical rain year of 17.7 inches, 370 Ortega Ridge Road receives approximately 40.3 million gallons of direct precipitation falling within property bounds. Of that amount, an estimated 8-20 million gallons (20-50%) is lost as runoff, depending on rainfall intensity and distribution throughout the rainy season."

> "At the Montecito Water District's current agricultural water rate of $.00760/gallon, this equates to $60,000 - $150,000 worth of water that is being lost annually."

### L2-C6 — Three-phase implementation sequencing
**Source:** L2 Whole Site Design, Section 2.1, p. 56

> "The site-wide passive water harvesting system has been divided up into three phases for the purposes of cost estimation and sequential implementation - more detail on these phases can be found in the document 'Level 3 - Passive Water Harvesting and Drainage System Phased Implementation Estimates'."

### L2-C7 — Erosion-control drainage recommendations
**Source:** L2 Whole Site Design, Section 2.2 Recommendations, p. 63

> "Installation of erosion-resistant drainage elements that include: geologically-armored level-sill spillways wherever overflow can either be captured by another downslope swale or infiltration basin... geologically-armored energy dissipation pools wherever concentrated flows will enter swales, infiltration basins, or existing drainages... 'Media Luna' flow spreaders wherever concentrated flows will drain onto a native slope... geologically-armored rundowns wherever concentrated flows must move downhill... rolling dips where concentrated flows will cross access roads that have grades greater than 3% and less than 15%... grade reversals (road humps) along the orchard roads to disperse drainage flows more uniformly across broader areas."

### L2-C8 — Road decommissioning
**Source:** L2 Whole Site Design, Section 3.1 Recommendations, p. 81

> "Decommissioning certain unnecessary, eroding and/or high risk segments of the low standard orchard access roads to eliminate the almost certain chance of future erosion damage."

> "In general, it is a good practice to keep roads out of valley bottoms where they are nearly impossible to drain effectively and thus represent a substantial erosion hazard."

> "To safely and effectively decommission the road segments, any berms trapping and funneling water into them should be knocked down, and road humps should be installed to cut off any potential inflows... The terrain should be returned to as natural a form as is feasible and be revegetated as soon as possible."

### L2-C9 — Avocado consolidation footprint
**Source:** L2 Whole Site Design, Section 5.2 Consolidation of Avocado Plantings, p. 106

> "Currently avocados are planted and actively growing on 43.2 acres at 370 Ortega Ridge Road. By consolidating some of the more sparsely planted and farthest afield avocado blocks... the avocado footprint can be consolidated down to approximately 27.3 acres, a 36.8% reduction in the landscape area footprint dedicated to avocados as the primary crop."

> "Reduce water use by creating a smaller footprint for the water-demanding avocado crop."

### L2-C10 — Food production system redesign
**Source:** L2 Whole Site Design, Section 5.2 Recommendations, p. 105

> "Consolidation of avocado production to smaller footprint primarily on the southwest side of the Main Ridge."

> "Removal and replacement of unprofitable lemon blocks with avocado or dryland/Mediterranean agroforestry guilds."

> "Establishment of Trial Planting Zones immediately surrounding the Ranch HQ and worker housing for species trials, guild testing, monitoring etc."

> "Multi-species food forest around the Main Residence for home-use."

### L2-C11 — Soil fertility and livestock integration
**Source:** L2 Whole Site Design, Executive Summary p. 10; Section 5.1, p. 95–97

> "Soil fertility-boosting recommendations that include cover-cropping in the understory of the orchards, livestock integration, and farm-scale nutrient cycling systems like flow-through vermicomposters (Section 5.1). Proper installation and management of these elements will also contribute to greater water holding capacity in the soils."

> "Due to the degraded conditions of the soils across the majority of the property, seeding in a dryland-adapted cover crop will help to create the initial soil cover layer to prevent raindrop erosion, increase infiltration, decrease soil surface temperatures and inject some carbon in the soil surface layers to begin feeding a diverse and healthy soil food web."

> "We recommend hiring an existing herd and manager as a way to trial the presence and impact of goats and/or sheep on the land."

> "TOXIC: Avocado leaves are toxic to goats and sheep alike... goats and sheep will need to be excluded from the avocado blocks."

### L2-C12 — Enterprise potentials and current crop economics
**Source:** L2 Whole Site Design, Section 7.1 Enterprise Potentials, p. 131

> "Mono-cropped orchard systems for avocados and lemons are the primary economic drivers of the ranch at this time."

> "Lemons are at best a break even crop, avocados carry the load during good years."

> "The 1.2 acres of passionfruit vines have proven to be the most profitable crop per acre during the past few years."

> "The economic viability of the .9 acres of dragonfruit is unknown at this time."
