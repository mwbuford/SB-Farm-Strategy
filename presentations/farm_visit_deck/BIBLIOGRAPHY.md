## A. Organic transition — revenue & market

1. Organic Produce Network. (2025). *California’s 2025 organic avocado harvest could reach record-high volume.*  
   https://www.organicproducenetwork.com/organic-retail/california-organic-avocado-harvest-might-be-biggest-yet  
   **Key finding:** ~10% of CA avocado crop estimated organic; conventional FOB ~$60–70/box with **~15% organic premium** (often higher when conventional prices are lower); U.S. organic avocado volume sales **+19% vs 2023**.

2. California Department of Food and Agriculture (CDFA), State Organic Program. (2024). *California Agricultural Organics Report 2023–2024.*  
   https://www.cdfa.ca.gov/Statistics/PDFs/2023-2024_california_agricultural_organics_report.pdf  
   **Key finding:** Statewide organic producer gross sales ~$4.6B (2023); documents county-level organic activity including Santa Barbara and Ventura.

3. California Avocado Commission / Land IQ. (2025). *2025 Statewide Avocado Mapping Report.*  
   https://www.californiaavocadogrowers.com/sites/default/files/2025-11/2025_Statewide_AvocadoMapping_Report.pdf  
   **Key finding:** **55,484** planted avocado acres statewide (2025); Santa Barbara County among top five producing counties (**6,844 acres** mapped).

4. California Avocado Commission. (2024). *2024–25 Business Plan.*  
   https://www.californiaavocadogrowers.com/sites/default/files/2025-01/2024-25-CAC-Business-Plan-10-10-24-FINAL%20(1).pdf  
   **Key finding:** Industry strategy prioritizes California brand premium vs. imports.

5. Klonsky, K. *Comparison of Production Costs and Resource Use for Organic and Conventional Production Systems* (UC Davis / NRCS).  
   https://cail.ucdavis.edu/publications/NRCSKlonskypaper.pdf  
   **Key finding:** Organic profitability often depends on premiums; cover-crop costs commonly ~$40–120/acre; weed management and fertility substitutes raise costs vs. conventional for many crops.

6. UC ANR / stakeholder report. (2024). *Challenges in Organic Agriculture in California.*  
   https://ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2024-04/396228.pdf  
   **Key finding:** Weeds, water, and labor are top organic production challenges; integrity and small-grower processor access remain issues.

6a. UCCE Ventura. *Avocado Pest Management.*  
    https://ucanr.edu/county/cooperative-extension-ventura-county/avocado-pest-management  
    **Key finding:** CA avocado orchards are generally under good biological control; protect predators/parasites; avoid unnecessary sprays that disrupt beneficials; consider releases (e.g. Trichogramma, lacewings) and Bt when needed.

6b. UC IPM. *Pest Management Guidelines: Avocado* (organically acceptable methods).  
    https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/avocado/  
    **Key finding:** Organic options emphasize biological + cultural controls; Bt or spinosad for caterpillars; dust control protects natural enemies; monitoring before treatment.

6c. Takele, E. et al. (UC). *Sample Costs to Establish and Produce Organic Avocados — Ventura, Santa Barbara, and San Luis Obispo Counties.*  
    https://coststudyfiles.ucdavis.edu/uploads/cs_public/f4/ba/f4bab2ce-ed5f-4103-a412-d472b9c7148d/avocadovsbsloorganicreport2011.pdf  
    **Key finding:** Organic thrips program example: spinosad (Entrust) + NR415 oil; annual oil for thrips/persea mites; consult certifier for allowed materials.

## B. Organic & regenerative — soil health

7. Rowe-Fish, A., & Faber, B. (UC Cooperative Extension). *Avocados and Cover Crops.* Topics in Subtropics.  
   https://ucanr.edu/blog/topics-subtropics/article/avocados-and-cover-crops  
   **Key finding:** Cover crops in CA avocado orchards improve structure, reduce compaction/erosion, raise organic matter, improve infiltration & moisture storage, support beneficials, and aid salt leaching via better rainfall capture.

8. California Avocado Commission. (Fall 2019; archived 2023). *Panel Explores Avocado Cover Crops.*  
   https://www.californiaavocadogrowers.com/sites/default/files/2023-11/10-Panel-Explores-Avocado-Cover-Crops-Fall-2019.pdf  
   **Key finding:** Ventura-area growers report dramatic runoff reduction after cover cropping; soil organic matter can rise substantially over years of practice (example cited from ~2% baseline).

9. UC Cooperative Extension Ventura County. *Cover Crops. A Love Story.* / *Ventura Grown Cover Crops.*  
   https://ucanr.edu/blog/uc-cooperative-extension-ventura-county/article/cover-crops-love-story  
   https://ucanr.edu/blog/uc-cooperative-extension-ventura-county/article/ventura-grown-cover-crops  
   **Key finding:** Winter establishment on rainfall; terminate before summer irrigation competition; daikon-type roots improve infiltration; Petty Ranch (~50 ac) cover-crops between lemons, avocados, figs.

10. UC ANR. *California Cover Crops Resources — Benefits.*  
    https://www.ucanr.edu/site/california-cover-crops-resources/benefits  
    **Key finding:** Hillside studies show runoff cuts of ~23–77% and erosion reductions ~50–75% vs. bare/tilled management (vineyard literature synthesized for CA).

11. California Avocado Growers Association. *Resource Center* (organic certification + Restore Grant notes).  
    https://www.californiaavocadogrowers.com/growing-california-avocados/resource-center  
    **Key finding:** Points growers to organic certification pathways and conservation grants (e.g., Zero Foodprint Restore Grant up to $25,000 for cover crops, mulching, hedgerows, grazing).

## C. Public funding for practices seen on farm visits

12. CDFA. *Healthy Soils Program (HSP).*  
    https://www.cdfa.ca.gov/oefi/healthysoils/  
    **Key finding:** Funds cover crops, compost, mulching, hedgerow planting, no-till/reduced till, etc. Program shifting to regional Block Grant model; monitor for SB/Ventura access.

13. CDFA. (2024). *Healthy Soils Program Incentive Grants — Request for Grant Applications* (eligible practices list).  
    https://www.cdfa.ca.gov/oefi/healthysoils/docs/2024/2024_hsp_incentives_rga.pdf  
    **Key finding:** Orchard/vineyard practices explicitly include Cover Crop (CPS 340), Hedgerow Planting (CPS 422), Mulching (CPS 484), Compost Application (CPS 808).

28. Zero Foodprint. *Restore Grants* + *Compost Connector*.  
    https://www.zerofoodprint.org/apply · https://www.zerofoodprint.org/compost  
    **Key finding:** Restore = up to **$25,000** for regenerative practices (cover, mulch, compost, hedgerows); scored on CO₂/$ via COMET; reopen **Fall 2026**. Compost Connector = CA compost rebates up to **~$20,000/year** from approved facilities (SB1383 jurisdiction funding). Also: California Avocado Growers Assoc. Restore Grant announcement listing eligible practices.

29. CDFA. *State Water Efficiency & Enhancement Program (SWEEP)* — Block Grant model.  
    https://www.cdfa.ca.gov/oars/sweep/ · https://www.cdfa.ca.gov/oefi/sweep/  
    **Key finding:** Funds irrigation scheduling, sensors, drip upgrades, pumps/VFDs, fuel conversion. Farmer apps closed; on-farm awards via regional Block Grant Recipients expected after ~2027 execution (historical on-farm caps ~$200k).

30. USDA NRCS. *Environmental Quality Incentives Program (EQIP) — California.*  
    https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/programs-initiatives/environmental-quality-incentives-program/california/environmental-quality  
    **Key finding:** Year-round applications; cost-share for conservation-plan practices including cover crops, hedgerows, mulch, irrigation efficiency; ranked against state cutoff dates. Pair with local USDA Service Center (SB/Ventura).

## D. Southern California peer operations — crops & models

14. Earl’s Organic Produce. (2022). *Farm Tour 2022: Rincon Avocados.*  
    https://www.earlsorganic.com/blog/farm-tour-2022-rincon-avocados  
    **Key finding:** Carpinteria packing hub; hillside organic avocados; diversification toward Mediterranean/lower-water crops (figs, persimmons, dragonfruit, agave) as water strategy.

15. Permanent.ag farm profile. *Rincon Farms.*  
    https://app.permanent.ag/farms/rincon-farms  
    **Key finding:** Cover crops, native+fruit hedgerows, packing for regional organic/conventional avocados; grant-supported hedgerows.

16. Levi’s Organic Avocados. *The Ranch* (Somis, CA).  
    https://levisorganicavocados.com/pages/the-ranch  
    **Key finding:** ~**240 acres**, certified organic since 1980s, **17 varieties**, soil-moisture sensors + efficient irrigation, direct-to-consumer packing/shipping model.

17. California Grown. *Fairfield Farms: An Organic Avocado + Blueberry Farm* (Pauma Valley / San Diego County).  
    https://californiagrown.org/blog/blueberry-farm/  
    **Key finding:** Mid/large organic avocado + blueberry diversification; drip + sensors; organic premium acknowledged as financial benefit alongside values-driven production.

18. Apricot Lane Farms. Official site.  
    https://www.apricotlanefarms.com/  
    **Key finding:** ~234 acres Moorpark; Certified Organic & Biodynamic; 200+ varieties marketed; animals, tours, farmers markets, nationwide avocado boxes.

19. Wanderment Farms. Official site + Field Notes; *Santa Barbara Independent* farm-tour feature (2024).  
    https://wandermentfarms.com/  
    https://wandermentfarms.com/pages/field-notes  
    https://www.independent.com/2024/05/15/farm-tour-gold-at-wanderment-farms/  
    **Key finding:** ~50–60 acre regenerative/organic coastal farm; cardboard+mulch weed control; olives, garden, avocados; agritourism + Friday boxes as revenue.

20. UC Cooperative Extension Ventura / Santa Barbara — Ben Faber (Soils, water, subtropical crops).  
    https://ucanr.edu/people/ben-faber  
    UCCE Ventura Annual Report 2024: https://ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2024-09/402040.pdf  
    **Key finding:** Extension focus on avocado/citrus plus cherimoya, passionfruit, dragonfruit for coastal SoCal specialty diversification.

21. UCCE. *Small Farms Network — San Luis Obispo & Santa Barbara Counties.*  
    https://ucanr.edu/county/county-san-luis-obispo/small-farms-network-san-luis-obispo-santa-barbara-counties  
    **Key finding:** Technical assistance hub for diversified/specialty small–mid farms in the region.

## D2. Lemon replacement economics and crop-fit references

22. UC ANR / UC Agricultural Issues Center. (2020). *Sample Costs to Establish and Produce Lemons in Ventura County* (Eureka lemons).  
    https://ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2020-11/338947.pdf  
    **Key finding:** Illustrates thin margin sensitivity in lemons; break-even around surveyed assumptions is tight and highly yield/price dependent.

23. UC ANR / UC Agricultural Issues Center. (2011 baseline report). *Avocado Sample Establishment and Production Costs and Profitability Analysis for Ventura, Santa Barbara, and San Luis Obispo Counties.*  
    https://ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2014-01/181271.pdf  
    **Key finding:** Provides Ventura/Santa Barbara avocado cost/return framework for comparing block-level replacement scenarios.

24. USDA AMS. *National FOB Review* (lemons, South/Central California shipping point reports; rolling weekly market references).  
    https://esmis.nal.usda.gov/sites/default/release-files/1g05fb620/h702s608p/5q47tn380/FVDFOB.PDF  
    **Key finding:** Size-grade lemon price bands are volatile; useful for trend context but not direct grower net returns.

25. California Citrus Mutual. (2025 weekly market memos).  
    https://www.cacitrusmutual.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/4834.pdf  
    https://www.cacitrusmutual.com/2025/08/14/week-33-august-14-2025/  
    https://www.cacitrusmutual.com/2025/09/25/week-39-september-25-2025/  
    **Key finding:** Confirms steady-but-tight lemon market pattern and clarifies USDA price references are trend indicators, not grower settlement returns.

26. UCCE Ventura County crop pages (coastal fit): *Cherimoya*, *Passion Fruit*, *Pitahaya/Dragon Fruit*.  
    https://ucanr.edu/county/cooperative-extension-ventura-county/cherimoya  
    https://ucanr.edu/county/cooperative-extension-ventura-county/passion-fruit  
    https://ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2017-11/274663.pdf  
    **Key finding:** Cherimoya and dragon fruit show strong coastal SoCal fit under proper management; passionfruit has market and disease caveats.

## D3. People, UCSB partnerships & living-lab models

31. UCSB Environmental Studies. *Internship Program (ENV S 192).*  
    https://es.ucsb.edu/internships  
    **Key finding:** Long-running credit internship pathway for ES majors; farm can host a paid/credit student coordinator under clear scope.

32. UCSB Bren School. *Bren Environmental Leadership (BEL) Program.*  
    https://bren.ucsb.edu/undergrad-mentoring/bren-environmental-leadership-bel-program  
    **Key finding:** Paid summer model pairing Bren graduate mentor + undergraduate with external partners on applied sustainability projects (prior ag partners include White Buffalo / Jalama-style regen work).

33. White Buffalo Land Trust. *Jalama Canyon Ranch — Center for Regenerative Agriculture* (+ SB Independent coverage of Summerland flagship living-lab narrative).  
    https://www.whitebuffalolandtrust.org/jalama-canyon-ranch  
    https://www.independent.com/2021/05/26/farming-for-the-future-at-jalama-canyon-ranch/  
    **Key finding:** Local regen education/research model; useful as knowledge-exchange or partnership template for transforming a coastal farm with university-linked programming — not an automatic ownership path.
