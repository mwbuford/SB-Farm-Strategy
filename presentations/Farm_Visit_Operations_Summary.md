## 1. Rincon Farms (Carpinteria)

**Website:** [rinconfarms.com](https://www.rinconfarms.com/)

### What they are
A family-run Carpinteria avocado operation with an **on-site packing house** that packs their own fruit and consolidates avocados (and exotics) from neighboring growers for wholesale and local sales. The pack shed is a regional hub, not only an on-farm amenity.

### Operations observed / documented

**Orchard & land**
- Avocado groves in coastal valleys; GAP-certified packing/handling standards.
- **Cover cropping** in avocado orchards — wildflowers and clovers seeded under/among trees to:
  - improve infiltration and water retention  
  - build organic matter and soil biology  
  - support pollinators and pest balance  
  - compete with weeds / break hardpan  
- **Hedgerows** along roads and edges (native shrubs + mixed fruit trees) for **erosion control**, wind/buffer, and biodiversity — directly relevant to Summerland’s road-channeled runoff problem.
- Smaller / pruned tree management discussed publicly as a water- and harvest-efficiency play.

**Packing & market**
- On-site packing for **avocados and exotics**.
- Supplies wholesale channels (e.g. Bay Area / LA) and local restaurants; packs both organic and conventional Carpinteria fruit under the Rincon label.
- Diversification underway (dragonfruit and other Mediterranean / subtropical crops) partly as a water/risk strategy.

### Why it matters for Summerland
| Practice | Transferability |
|---|---|
| Hedgerows on roads / drainage lines | **High** — addresses erosion Max flagged as #1 issue |
| Cover crops in avocados | **High** — test tradeoff vs irrigation cost (Sarah’s historical concern) |
| On-site / local packing for avocados & exotics | **Medium–high** for passionfruit & specialty fruit marketing (also compare Rincon Tropics packing model) |
| Packing as a service to neighbors | **Low near-term** unless scale and food-safety investment justify it |

**Visit takeaway:** Rincon is the closest **operational peer** for Summerland’s avocado hills — same crop, coastal SB County context, and practical regenerative steps that don’t require becoming a full livestock farm overnight.

## 2. Apricot Lane Farms (Moorpark)

**Website:** [apricotlanefarms.com](https://www.apricotlanefarms.com/)

### What they are
A ~234-acre **Certified Organic and Biodynamic®** regenerative farm founded 2011; subject of *The Biggest Little Farm*. Public brand emphasizes ecosystem restoration, soil health, and nutrient-dense food. Large permanent staff historically (~50 FTE noted in owner meeting); management team has changed since the film era.

### Operations observed / documented

**Crops & diversity**
- Markets **200+ varieties** of fruits and vegetables.
- Visit / meeting note: commercial portfolio has been **narrowed toward roughly 17 crop lines** — a useful signal of which specialties still pencil out at scale.
- Includes orchards (e.g. highly biodiverse “fruit basket”), garden blocks, avocados (incl. regenerative avocado boxes shipped nationally).

**Animals as management tools**
- Cattle, sheep, pigs, chickens, ducks, goats, guardian dogs.
- Grazing sequences (e.g. cattle on cover crop → chickens follow for insects/manure breakdown) — livestock are laborers, not only products.
- Pasture-raised meats and eggs sold via farmers markets and farm channels.

**Fertility system**
- Dedicated **Fertility Center**: large vermicompost (worm) system + **aerated compost tea** brewers + biodynamic preparations.
- Compost tea used as soil drench / foliar to spread microbial life from a small amount of high-quality compost.
- Closed-loop goal: fertility generated on-farm rather than imported synthetics.

**Habitat / pollinators**
- Land intentionally dedicated to **pollinators**, wildlife corridors, ponds, and native plant restoration (on the order of a meaningful share of acreage in habitat, not 100% production).
- Biodiversity framed as pest/disease “immunity” for the farm, not only conservation branding.

**Markets**
- Farmers markets (Santa Monica, Thousand Oaks, Santa Clarita, etc.).
- Nationwide avocado boxes / honey lemonade; farm tours and school field trips.

### Why it matters for Summerland
| Practice | Transferability |
|---|---|
| Compost tea / vermicompost fertility loop | **Medium** — start small; needs labor + consistent recipe (see their public compost guide) |
| Animals for understory / fertility | **Medium–long term** — needs fencing, management, and likely a regenerative-minded farm manager |
| Dedicated pollinator / habitat acres | **High as a phased idea** on ex-citrus or eroded edges |
| Narrowing to ~17 commercial crops | **High strategic lesson** for citrus transition / specialty planting choices |
| Full Apricot Lane staffing & brand model | **Low** — different capital, labor, and tourism footprint |

**Visit takeaway:** Apricot Lane is the **north star system** (animals + fertility + habitat + diversity), not a copy-paste ops plan. The actionable pieces are compost tea, habitat edges, and the crop-portfolio discipline (~17 lines).

## 3. Wanderment Farms (Carpinteria / Toro Canyon)

**Website:** [wandermentfarms.com](https://wandermentfarms.com/)

### What they are
A **smaller, local regenerative / certified organic** farm (order of ~50–60 acres) run by Margo & Jeff Redfern since ~2020 on former vineyard land above Carpinteria. Functionally a **compact cousin of Apricot Lane’s philosophy**, with a clearer near-term **profit mix from agritourism + weekly boxes**.

### Operations observed / documented

**Crops & land use**
- Market garden produce, **avocados**, **heritage olive grove** (~4 acres; estate olive oil), replanted vines, coffee and agave trials, native plant / pollinator areas.
- Large share of property described as managed for carbon / water benefits, not max production.

**Weed & soil management (highly practical)**
- **Cardboard + mulch** layered to smother weeds without herbicides — simple, no-till-friendly, builds organic matter as cardboard breaks down.
- Livestock grazed under olives (“fluff crew”) to manage understory and cycle fertility.
- Mobile chicken coops; chickens follow grazing animals in vineyard/orchard sequences (pest + manure processing), similar in spirit to Apricot Lane but at smaller scale.

**Animals & agritourism**
- Valais Blacknose sheep, Nigerian Dwarf goats, Highpark mini cows, Silkie chickens, llamas/donkeys — breeding + visitor appeal.
- **Agritourism is a real P&L line:** guided tours, private parties, corporate retreats, animal meet-and-greets — not only education.
- Friday customizable **produce boxes** (produce, eggs, bread, flowers) delivered to Santa Barbara / Montecito / Carpinteria customers.

### Why it matters for Summerland
| Practice | Transferability |
|---|---|
| Cardboard + mulch weed suppression | **High / quick win** on avocado blocks or ex-citrus edges |
| Agritourism as income | **Medium** — zoning, insurance, access, and brand; study before investing |
| CSA / Friday box model | **Medium** for passionfruit + specialty fruit if packing/logistics solved |
| Sheep/chicken rotation under trees | **Medium** — pilotable with clear fencing and manager oversight |
| “Small Apricot Lane” diversified lifestyle farm | **Inspiration** — Summerland’s 80 acres and avocado economics differ |

**Visit takeaway:** Wanderment is the **most copyable regenerative playbook** for Summerland in the near term: cardboard/mulch, ground cover, modest animal integration, and optional agritourism — without needing a 234-acre biodynamic campus.

## Cross-farm comparison (what to steal)

### Tier A — do soon at Summerland
1. **Hedgerows / vegetative buffers on roads and erosion channels** (Rincon)  
2. **Cover crop trial in selected avocado blocks** with water-use monitoring (Rincon → Apricot Lane methods)  
3. **Cardboard + mulch weed kill** on bare / eroded patches (Wanderment)  
4. **Pollinator strip or native edge** on a non-productive margin (Apricot Lane / Wanderment)

### Tier B — design for 1–3 years
5. Small **compost tea** or vermicompost pilot (Apricot Lane method, small equipment)  
6. Passionfruit / exotic **packing & channel** strategy informed by Rincon’s pack-house model (and Rincon Tropics pricing follow-up)  
7. Crop shortlist for ex-citrus acres using Apricot Lane’s “narrow to what sells” lesson (~17 lines)

### Tier C — needs farm manager / larger capital
8. Rotational livestock under orchard (Apricot Lane / Wanderment)  
9. Full agritourism program (Wanderment-style)  
10. On-farm packing house at Summerland scale (Rincon-style)

## One-line summaries for the owner deck

- **Rincon:** Coastal avocado peer with packing infrastructure; proving cover crops + road hedgerows on real hills.  
- **Apricot Lane:** Full regenerative ecosystem (animals, compost tea, pollinator land); teach the *system*, copy pieces carefully; heed the shift toward fewer commercial crops.  
- **Wanderment:** Local, profitable-enough regenerative mini-model — cardboard/mulch, boxes, and agritourism as cash flow while soil improves.
